# China → Europe Container Freight and Port Operations: Research Notes

Compiled 2026-09-15 via web search. Every fact below is sourced; numbers vary
across sources by methodology, so ranges are given where sources disagree.

## 1. Booking and sailing mechanics

- Advance booking of roughly 14-21 days is the industry-average lead time; booking early is associated with an 18-25% cost reduction versus late/spot booking. Source: [Container Booking: Definition & Guide for 2026](https://docshipper.com/glossary/container-booking-definition-logistics/)
- **Cut-off**: the final date/time for cargo/documentation changes before loading; missing it (or a later cancellation) typically triggers a roll/cancellation fee cited around $500/container in forwarder guidance. Source: same as above.
- **Transit time, Shanghai→Rotterdam via Suez (pre-2024 baseline)**: theoretical steaming time ~27 days at 16 knots over ~10,600 nautical miles with no port calls; real-world carrier schedules with port calls run **28-32 days**, with some guides citing a wider 30-45 day range depending on transshipment. Source: [Routing Options between Shanghai, Rotterdam and New York — Port Economics, Management and Policy](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part1/interoceanic-passages/routing-options-shanghai-rotterdam/)
- **Transit time via Cape of Good Hope**: adds roughly **10-14 days** versus the Suez routing, pushing total Asia-Europe transit to **40-50 days**; one Red-Sea-crisis analysis specifically states the diversion "extends journeys by around ten days, bringing the total time to 40 to 50 days." Source: [The Red Sea Shipping Crisis (2024-2025) — Atlas Institute](https://atlasinstitute.org/the-red-sea-shipping-crisis-2024-2025-houthi-attacks-and-global-trade-disruption/)
- A separate estimate: diversions extended transit from Southeast Asia to Europe by **33%**, and China to Europe specifically by **25%**. Source: same Atlas Institute article.
- **Schedule reliability (Sea-Intelligence Global Schedule Reliability Index)**: post-Red-Sea-crisis levels have run in the **59-68%** band through 2025-2026 (e.g., Aug 2025: 65.3%; Oct 2025: 61.4%; Nov 2025: 64.1%; Dec 2025: 62.8%; Jan 2026: 62.4%; Feb 2026: 59.0%). Best individual carriers in Mar 2026 reported Hapag-Lloyd 72.3%, Maersk 70.8%. Sources: [Sea-Intelligence press room — Dec 2025](https://sea-intelligence.com/press-room/368-global-schedule-reliability-drops-to-62-8-in-december-2025), [Jan 2026](https://www.sea-intelligence.com/press-room/373-2026-starts-with-global-schedule-reliability-of-62-4), [Feb 2026](https://www.sea-intelligence.com/press-room/379-february-2026-global-schedule-reliability-falls-to-59-0), [Nov 2025](https://www.sea-intelligence.com/press-room/364-global-schedule-reliability-improves-to-64-1-in-november-2025), [Mar 2026](https://sea-intelligence.com/press-room/384-march-2026-global-schedule-reliability-joint-highest-for-the-year)
- **Blank sailings**: voyage cancellations used by carriers to match capacity to demand, typically announced **2-6 weeks in advance** (shorter notice happens); a blank sailing adds roughly **1-2 weeks** of delay depending on service frequency. Source: [How carriers manage and use blank sailings — Shipping and Freight Resource](https://www.shippingandfreightresource.com/how-carriers-manage-and-use-blank-sailings/)
- **Rolled/shut-out cargo**: a booked container not loaded onto its scheduled vessel (overbooking, late arrival past cut-off, weight limits, inspection hold, carrier prioritizing higher-revenue cargo). A roll adds a minimum of **~1 week** (next sailing frequency), and **2+ weeks** on lower-frequency services. Source: same Shipping and Freight Resource article family; [Rolled Cargo Meaning — SeaFreightGo](https://seafreightgo.com/rolled-cargo-meaning-shipping-delays/)
- **Transshipment**: common on Asia-Europe strings via hubs (e.g., Tanjung Pelepas, Singapore, Port Said); adds dwell/handling time not counted in port-to-port direct transit figures — noted qualitatively across the routing-options and forwarder-guide sources above; no single authoritative added-day figure was found (treat as an extra 3-7 days pending a dedicated source).

## 2. Pricing

- **FBX/SCFI indices**: Freightos Baltic Index (FBX) is a daily 40ft-container spot rate index across 12 global tradelanes; FBX11 is specifically the China/East-Asia → North Europe lane (distinct from FBX01, which is China→US West Coast). Source: [Freightos Baltic Index — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freightos_Baltic_Index), [FBX-11 China to Northern Europe — Freightos Terminal](https://www.freightos.com/enterprise/terminal/fbx-11-china-to-northern-europe/)
- **2021 peak**: Shanghai-Rotterdam 40ft spot rate peaked at **$10,377** in September 2021. Source: [Shanghai-Rotterdam container rates: Lowest level of 2024 in sight](https://www.interiordaily.com/article/9663540/shanghai-rotterdam-container-rates-lowest-level-of-2024-in-sight/)
- **2024 Red Sea-driven rates**: Shanghai-Rotterdam 40ft rates were **$4,999** in May 2024, falling to **$4,172** (mid-Sept) and **$4,157** (late Sept) 2024 — still well below the 2021 peak but elevated versus pre-crisis norms; SCFI (composite index) hit **3,438.5** points in June 2024 and **3,650.4** in July 2024. Source: same interiordaily.com article; [Peak season comes early as container rates continue to soar — Seatrade Maritime](https://www.seatrade-maritime.com/containers/peak-season-comes-early-as-container-rates-continue-to-soar)
- General 2024 level: overall global ocean rates remained roughly **60% higher than 2019 levels** due to Red Sea diversions absorbing capacity market-wide. Source: [Freightos Weekly Update — AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/freightos-weekly-update-ocean-rates-reach-new-highs-with-additional-increases-expected-soon)
- **2025 correction**: by April 2025, FBX global rates had slipped *below* the 2024 floor. Source: [FBX Index April 2025: Global rates slip below 2024 floor — Baltic Exchange](https://www.balticexchange.com/en/news-and-events/market-information/container/2025/Global-rates-slip-below-2024-floor.html)
- **Contract vs spot**: annual service contracts on major lanes traditionally get fixed around **May 1** (the "May-to-May" contract season) with a hard stop by end-April/mid-May; shippers commonly split volume, e.g. a **70/30 contract/spot mix**, to hedge between rate certainty and market upside. Contracts fix a rate + minimum-volume commitment for 12 months, but compliance from both sides frequently breaks down when spot diverges sharply from the contract rate. Sources: [Ocean Freight Contract Season — FreightRight](https://www.freightright.com/news/ocean-freight-rates-2024), [Negotiating Freight Rates 101 — VIZION](https://www.vizionapi.com/blog/negotiating-freight-rates-101-short-term-annual-and-semi-annual)
- **Surcharges**:
  - Peak Season Surcharge (PSS): MSC announced **$500/20ft, $1,000/40ft** PSS increase effective Nov 9, 2024; transpacific PSS more generally ranges **$200-$1,000/FEU** in season. Source: [Peak Season Surcharge and Emergency Operation Surcharge — MSC](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2024/october/peak-season-surcharge-and-emergency-operation-surcharge)
  - BAF (Bunker Adjustment Factor): tracks fuel-cost fluctuation; Q1 2026 BAF on major east-west lanes ran **$400-$600/FEU**. Source: [Emergency Fuel Surcharges Explained 2026 — SeaRates](https://www.searates.com/blog/post/emergency-fuel-surcharges-explained-2026)
  - Emergency/War-Risk Surcharge (ERS): applied for Cape of Good Hope diversion / conflict-zone risk, roughly **$200-$800/container** on top of base freight. Source: same SeaRates article.
  - Cape of Good Hope rerouting also carries an operating-cost premium separately estimated at **~$1 million per voyage** (extra fuel/time). Source: [The Red Sea Shipping Crisis — Atlas Institute](https://atlasinstitute.org/the-red-sea-shipping-crisis-2024-2025-houthi-attacks-and-global-trade-disruption/)

## 3. Port side: free time, demurrage/detention, congestion

- **Free time**: standard free time before charges start is typically **3-7 days for demurrage** and **4-7 days for detention**, varying by carrier/port/trade lane and local congestion (e.g., a carrier might grant 5 free days at Rotterdam vs 3 at Newark). Source: [Free Days & Demurrage Explained — TraceContainer](https://tracecontainer.com/blog/free-days-demurrage-explained-the-complete-2026-guide/)
- **Demurrage/detention daily rates, Rotterdam**: roughly **$40-$80/day**; carrier-specific 2020 data: Maersk **$80.4/day**, CMA CGM **$51.8/day**, Cosco **$43.6/day**. Source: [Demurrage & detention charges in Rotterdam 2020 — Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1127439/average-demurrage-and-detention-charges-in-rotterdam-by-shipping-line/)
- **Hamburg**: notably higher — one carrier's cumulative charge reached **$3,498 after 14 days**. General industry framing: fees often run **$100-$150/container/day** once past free time, tiered upward the longer the delay. Source: [Demurrage and detention charges: Save money in top 10 ports — xChange](https://www.container-xchange.com/blog/demurrage-detention)
- **Customs clearance (Netherlands/Rotterdam)**: standard clearance **24-48 hours** with complete documentation; complex cases **3-5 days**; physical scans take **1-4 hours** plus ~1 day logistics; Rotterdam/Antwerp customs operate **24/7**. Source: [Dutch customs clearance: practical steps for importers](https://gettransport.com/articles/importers-simplify-customs-clearance-netherlands), [Rotterdam Port Customs Inspection — iContainers](https://www.icontainers.com/help/rotterdam-port-customs-inspection/)
- **LA/Long Beach 2021 congestion**: ship queue hit a record of roughly **70-100 vessels** waiting (56 record in Sept, then 70, peaking near 100 by October 2021); average anchorage-to-berth wait reached **8.5-8.7 days** at the peak. Sources: [Container ship queue at LA/Long Beach grows to record 60 vessels — S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/platts/en/market-insights/latest-news/shipping/091521-container-ship-queue-at-los-angeleslong-beach-grows-to-record-60-vessels), [Los Angeles Port Logjam Tops 50 Ships; Wait Exceeds Eight Days — Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2021-09-11/los-angeles-port-logjam-tops-50-ships-wait-exceeds-eight-days)
- **Felixstowe strikes, 2022**: two separate 8-day strikes (Aug 21-29 and starting Sept 27), 1,900 Unite union dockworkers; import vessel berth-wait nearly doubled to **~10 days**; export containers waited as long as **21 days**; waiting TEU capacity off port limits rose to **>65,500 TEU** (from a normal 20,000-30,000 TEU) by Sept 30; trade value at stake was estimated at **$4.7 billion** for the eight-day August strike. Sources: [$4.7 billion in trade to be delayed in eight-day strike at Port of Felixstowe — CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/08/23/4point7-billion-in-trade-delayed-in-eight-day-strike-at-felixstowe.html), [Export Cargo Wait at Felixstowe Port Jumps Near 10 Days — Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2022-08-29/supply-chain-latest-felixstowe-port-strike)
- **Rotterdam/Antwerp/Hamburg congestion, 2025-2026** (illustrative of recurring dynamics, not 2024 specifically — no strong 2024-specific figures were found): Rotterdam World Gateway berth waits up to **10 days** (Apr 2026); Antwerp/Rotterdam-Antwerp region waiting times rose **37%** (32→44 hrs, Apr-May 2025) at Antwerp and **49%** at Hamburg, **77%** at Bremerhaven over the same window; Antwerp dwell times exceeded **8 days** with barge queues of **72-75 hours** at Rotterdam/Antwerp — officials called it the worst congestion since COVID-19. Source: [How Bad Is European Port Congestion Really in Late 2025? — Tradlinx](https://blogs.tradlinx.com/how-bad-is-european-port-congestion-really-in-late-2025/), [European Port Congestion is 'Here to Stay' Through 2025 — WWD Sourcing Journal](https://wwd.com/sourcing-journal/logistics/europe-port-congestion-rotterdam-antwerp-hmm-hamburg-ocean-carriers-freight-rates-container-shipping-1238854373/)

## 4. Disruption case data

- **Ever Given, Suez Canal, March 2021**: grounded **March 23**, freed **March 29** — a **6-day** blockage. Queue reached **422 ships** waiting on both sides (some reports cite 400+). Estimated **$9 billion/day** in global trade held up. Maersk alone reported a loss of ~**$89 million**. Allianz Research estimated the closure could curtail global trade by **0.2-0.4% per annum**. Source: [2021 Suez Canal obstruction — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Suez_Canal_obstruction), [The cost of the Suez Canal blockage — University of Gothenburg](https://www.gu.se/en/news/the-cost-of-the-suez-canal-blockage), [Suez Canal Traffic Backlog Finally Cleared — NPR](https://www.npr.org/2021/04/03/984111501/suez-canal-traffic-backlog-finally-cleared-following-the-ever-given-saga)
- **Red Sea/Bab-el-Mandeb crisis, late 2023-2025**:
  - Container vessel traffic through Suez dropped **~75%** in 2024 vs 2023, and Q4 2025 transits were down **86%** vs 2023 — reduced levels persisted through 2025 with no recovery as of mid/late 2025. Source: [Suez Canal Traffic Stalls at 60% Below Normal Despite 100 Days Without Houthi Attacks — gCaptain](https://gcaptain.com/suez-canal-traffic-stalls-at-60-below-normal-despite-100-days-without-houthi-attacks/)
  - Suez Canal's share of global maritime traffic fell from **12% to 9%** since the 2023 attacks began. Source: [The Red Sea crisis: Renewed attacks keep shipping at risk — project44](https://www.project44.com/supply-chain-insights/the-red-sea-crisis-ceasefire-collapse-leaves-red-sea-in-tumultuous-state/)
  - In a normal year the Suez Canal handles roughly **50 vessels/day** (IMF PortWatch baseline); Cape of Good Hope diversion adds **~10-14 days** and roughly **$1 million/voyage**. Source: [IMF PortWatch — Data & Methodology](https://portwatch.imf.org/pages/data-and-methodology), [MacroMicro — IMF Suez Canal Daily Transit Calls](https://en.macromicro.me/charts/94483/imf-suez-canal-number-number-of-ships-and-transit-volume)
  - Diversion decision propagation: major carriers (Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd) began diverting Asia-Europe strings via the Cape within **days to a few weeks** of the December 2023 attacks escalating — by early 2024 Bab-el-Mandeb transits had fallen to about **half** prior volumes, i.e. the fleet-wide rerouting decision propagated on the order of **weeks, not months**. Source: [Routing Options — Port Economics, Management and Policy](https://porteconomicsmanagement.org/pemp/contents/part1/interoceanic-passages/routing-options-shanghai-rotterdam/) (search-derived synthesis; treat the "weeks" figure as approximate pending a single authoritative timeline source)
- **Baltimore Francis Scott Key Bridge collapse, March 26, 2024**: struck by container ship **Dali** (10,000-TEU capacity, en route to Colombo); port traffic suspended indefinitely immediately after; shipping channel debris cleared and channel operational by **late May 2024** (~2 months); full recovery cited as taking **~3 months**. Port of Baltimore handled **793,000 loaded TEU** in 2023 (small versus Port of NY/NJ's 5M+ TEU), and diverted cargo went mainly to NY/NJ and Norfolk, VA. Estimated regional economic loss **$15 million/day**. Sources: [Logistics companies scramble after bridge collapse — CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/26/logistics-companies-scramble-after-bridge-collapse-closes-port-of-baltimore.html), [Baltimore bridge: Economy could lose $15M daily — The Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2024/03/26/bridge-collapse-likely-to-have-profound-impact-on-port-of-baltimore-operations/), [Recovery and challenges — FleetOwner](https://www.fleetowner.com/news/article/55249102/recovery-and-challenges-the-impact-of-the-francis-scott-key-bridge-collapse-on-baltimores-port-and-freight-traffic)

## 5. Air freight as an alternative

- **Cost**: typical China→Europe air freight spot rates run **$4-$9/kg** for standard cargo (some sources widen the band to $4-$15/kg depending on volume/speed/season). Source: [Current Air Freight Rates from China | 2025 Pricing Guide — Top China Freight](https://topchinafreight.com/current-air-freight-rates-from-china-2025-pricing-guide/)
- **Transit time**: **5-8 days** door-to-door/airport-to-airport, versus **30-45 days** by ocean — the standard trigger for mode-switch when a shipper needs speed. Source: same Top China Freight guide; [Air Freight Rates from China to Europe — Top China Freight](https://topchinafreight.com/air-freight-rates-from-china-to-europe-cost-guide/)
- **Sea-air hybrid**: typical routing is ocean China→Singapore (or Colombo) then air Singapore/Dubai→Europe; total transit **~20-22 days** for the Europe-via-Singapore path, or **12-18 days** for China-Dubai specifically; cost is roughly **40-60% of pure air freight per kg** (~$3.50/kg cited to Europe). Best fit: mid-to-high value goods, shipments **>2 CBM**, acceptable lead time **18-25 days** — pure air below ~10 days needed, pure ocean if 35+ days is acceptable. Source: [Sea Air Cargoes: The 2026 B2B Expert's Guide — LooperBuy](https://looperbuy.com/blog/sea-air-cargoes-the-2026-b2b-experts-guide-to-hybrid-shipping-from-china.html)

## Implications for a simulator

- **Two-mode transit-time distribution, not a single mean**: model ocean transit as roughly Normal/lognormal around 28-32 days in a "Suez-open" regime and a distinct, higher-mean, higher-variance regime (40-50 days) when a Red-Sea-style diversion is active — these are two different regimes with a discrete regime-switch, not one continuously-drifting parameter.
- **Schedule reliability as a stochastic per-voyage miss probability**: the Sea-Intelligence 59-68% band implies roughly a third to two-fifths of voyages arrive off-schedule even in "normal" post-2024 operation — bake in a per-leg on-time probability around 0.6-0.65 as the baseline, not 0.9+.
- **Blank sailing / roll as a discrete, catchable-in-advance shock with 2-6 week lead time**: these should be modelled as an agent-observable-with-delay event (announced ahead) rather than a surprise, whereas Ever-Given/bridge-collapse-style chokepoint closures are true zero-notice shocks.
- **Rate regime should be a slow-moving latent state, spot a fast noisy observation of it**: contract rates fix once a year (May) and are sticky; spot (FBX/SCFI-like) can move 2-3x within months (e.g., $4,157 to $10,377 40ft range 2021-2024) — a POMDP belief over "rate regime" (normal/elevated/crisis) driving both spot-price mean and diversion probability captures this cleanly.
- **Demurrage/detention as a per-day penalty with a free-time grace window**: use a grace period of ~5 days then a penalty on the order of $50-100/container/day, escalating (tiered) with dwell time — matches Rotterdam/Hamburg data and is a natural lever for "clear the box fast."
- **Congestion should correlate with recent disruption state**, not be i.i.d.: 2022 Felixstowe and 2025 Rotterdam/Antwerp data both show congestion metrics (wait days, queued TEU) that build up over 1-2 months and take a similar multi-week window to unwind — a queue/backlog state variable with slow decay is more realistic than a memoryless congestion draw each period.
- **Air / sea-air as a real escape valve with a clear switch rule**: give the agent an air-freight option priced ~15-30x the per-kg ocean rate but with 5-8 day transit (vs 28-50 days), and a middle sea-air option at ~2-3x ocean cost with ~20 day transit — this creates a genuine three-way tradeoff (cost/speed/reliability) rather than a binary choice, and mirrors real shipper behavior only kicking in at high stockout urgency or high per-unit value.
- **Chokepoint closures are rare, discrete, high-impact, and propagate with a lag**: Ever Given-style total blockage (single-digit days, near-total capacity loss on the route) versus Red-Sea-style partial diversion (persistent, 75-86% traffic reduction on the direct route, but capacity absorbed elsewhere within weeks) are different tail-risk shapes worth encoding as separate rare events rather than one generic "port closed" flag.
