Five disruption scenarios, each played by GPT-5.6 Luna through tool calls and by a rule-based planner from the same starting point with the same hidden events. Ten runs, all complete. Pick a run above to step through it day by day. The full evidence, with the day, the tool call and the engine's reply for every item, is in docs/model-findings.md in the repository.

Where the model went wrong

  • Capacity blindness. On day one of the Red Sea run it released work orders for 35,000 units against a plant that makes 4,000 a day, using up most of the component stock in one move. It did the same at the start of the CNY run.
  • No replenishment. In the 60-day CNY scenario it placed no purchase orders at all, although its briefing named the shutdown. The rule planner placed 37.
  • Ignoring what was on the screen. It re-expedited orders already marked expedited, tried to buy from a supplier the briefing marked as unqualified, and allocated quantities the inventory screen showed were not there.
  • Fighting the engine. Manual allocations collided with the daily automatic allocation; one carried a quantity that contradicted itself.
  • No freight. In the final invoice-mismatch run it never booked a container in 45 days. Late and short deliveries went from 1 and 15 (rule) to 27 and 44.

What it did well

  • Read the Cape diversion notice on the day it appeared and adjusted.
  • Reacted quickly and correctly when the chip supplier went insolvent.
  • Disputed the over-billed invoice the day it became visible.

What the runs found in the simulator

Four bugs: two document types never closed, so the records screen grew without bound; the evidence summary was not saved with the run; and two scenario events could silently fail to fire for a player who had not yet created the document they targeted. All four are fixed. The affected scenario was re-run and the earlier runs are kept under a revision suffix.
