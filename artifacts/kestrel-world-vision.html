Interactive concept brief · Proposed direction

A small economy.
A world of consequences.

Turn a company simulator into a place where companies have lives of their own. Suppliers make commitments. Carriers fill capacity. Customers find alternatives. Your decisions change what happens next.

The central idea

Persistent companies respond to decisions. Interesting situations emerge from conflicts over real resources.

A product vision to explore.
The world mechanisms below are proposed.
01 / Existing foundation

A working company to build from.

A daily operating engine

3 products · 5 suppliers
One plant · 3 distribution centers

Connected business records

Record lifecycles, inventory and cash connect actions to outcomes.

Multiple ways to play

Human, rule-based and LLM interfaces, with replayable decisions.

02 / Proposed small economy

Give the other side a life.

Every counterparty has something to protect, a limit it cannot ignore, and a reason to respond. Select a participant to explore.

An actor can use explicit rules, an algorithm, a person or an AI policy. It does not need an LLM to have capacity, commitments and economic responses. The factory can begin as an internal operator before becoming a shared counterparty.

Physical truth

What exists: stock, capacity, shipments and cash. The engine validates constraints before any action changes state.

Commitments

What is promised: quantities, slots, prices and due dates. A reservation changes availability; a promise is not a delivery.

Evidence

What a player can know: orders, invoices, status updates and receipts. Documents expose or request actions; prose never decides physical outcomes.

03 / A decision travels

One choice. Several consequences.

Explore possible causal chains. These are design hypotheses, not measured simulator outputs or a claim that one policy wins.

Shared starting point

A customer wants an earlier delivery. Supplier availability, factory slots and carrier space are all limited.

04 / Kestrel World

From running a company
to inhabiting a world.

The larger opportunity is a reusable place to operate, experiment and compare decisions—across companies that share the same economy.

Configure the economy

Choose companies, products, networks and starting conditions. Make a compact teaching world or a richer operating environment.

Choose who plays

Let humans, algorithms and AI agents operate through the same validated actions, with explicit information access and rules.

Share counterparties

Two companies can seek the same supplier allocation or carrier slot. Their decisions become part of each other’s environment.

Reuse a living world

Return to persistent company histories or replay a saved starting state. An optional frozen slice can support a benchmark.

05 / A plausible path

Deepen first. Then open the world.

A sequence of experiments, not a delivery commitment. Each step should earn the next through observable behavior.

01

Deepen one economy

Give existing counterparties persistent state, capacity and commitments. Let an ordinary purchasing decision change their next response.

Look for: a replayable chain from decision to counterparty response to operating outcome.

02

Add competing counterparties

Introduce alternative buyers and providers that compete for shared resources. Let congestion and switching follow from their choices.

Look for: resource conflicts that produce interesting situations without a scripted crisis.

03

Make worlds configurable

Package company networks, initial states and player roles into reusable worlds for demos, training and research.

Look for: a different world using the same engine, with inspectable histories and reproducible starts.

Manager takeaway

Build an economy people and agents can operate inside. Start with one company whose counterparties remember, commit and respond.

Scope boundary. This is a vision artifact, not an implemented world or an approved specification. Preserve the submitted STOCKTAKE benchmark, research artifacts and scoring. Any future world benchmark should be a separate, explicitly frozen configuration. Scenario assumptions and illustrative behaviors must be documented separately from industry evidence.
